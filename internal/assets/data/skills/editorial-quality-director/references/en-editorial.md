# English editorial reference

Load this reference only when the dominant prose language is English or the user requests English editorial normalization.

## Preserve meaning

Keep exact:

- names;
- numbers;
- dates;
- measurements;
- code;
- commands;
- API symbols;
- filenames/paths;
- quotations;
- citations;
- contractual wording;
- defined terminology.

Do not trade precision for idiomatic smoothness.

## Sentence clarity

Check:

- clear grammatical subject;
- modifiers close to their targets;
- parallel construction where useful;
- pronoun antecedents;
- needless nominalization;
- repeated sentence openings;
- stacked prepositional phrases;
- long noun strings;
- excessive parenthetical interruptions.

Use passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally deemphasized.

## Concision

Cut:

- throat-clearing;
- duplicated conclusions;
- empty qualifiers;
- generic intensifiers;
- transitions that add no logical relationship;
- redundant restatement after a clear list/table.

Do not remove necessary caveats merely to shorten the text.

## Punctuation / house style

Preserve an established house style.

Do not automatically enforce:

- Oxford comma;
- em dash vs spaced en dash;
- title case vs sentence case;
- US vs UK spelling.

Normalize only if inconsistency harms quality or a house style is specified.

## Technical English

Preserve modal strength:

- MUST = mandatory;
- SHOULD = recommended;
- MAY = optional.

Do not replace these casually in normative documentation.

Prefer executable instructions with explicit prerequisites and expected outcomes.

## Anti-corporate-slop checks

Watch for:

- "unlock";
- "leverage" when "use" is clearer;
- "seamless";
- "robust" without evidence;
- "cutting-edge";
- "transformative";
- "in today's fast-paced world";
- "it is important to note";
- generic "key takeaway" summaries;
- repetitive three-part rhetorical structures.

Do not ban these words categorically. Keep them if they are specific and justified.

## Final English pass

Confirm:

- no accidental US/UK spelling drift unless normalized intentionally;
- no identifier changed;
- no number/unit changed;
- punctuation is consistent;
- prose does not sound translated unless that is the intended register;
- voice remains recognizable.
